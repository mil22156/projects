# Bathroom Renovation

## Overview

Ward's downstairs bathroom. Wife has a separate upstairs bathroom — not a focus of this project.
Primary motivation: replace the current bathtub with something larger and better for soaking.

---

## Bathtub

### Constraints
- Alcove length: **60"** (fixed)
- Alcove depth available: **43"**
- Minimum tub width: **32"**; targeting **36"** for standing room and comfort
- Practical maximum width: ~38–39" (leaves room for surround/tile)

### Preferences
- Style: refined but not ornate — clean lines, minimal detailing
- Soaking depth: targeting 18–20" interior depth
- Budget: up to **$2,500**; willing to consider up to **$3,000** for the right tub

### Notes
- Continuous/tankless water heater — not a constraint for large fill volume
- Water usage: no hard limit but wants to be reasonable (a 60"x36" soaking tub runs ~80–100 gallons, comparable to a long shower)
- Standard 30" tub holds ~40–60 gallons; stepping up to 36" wide + soaking depth is a meaningful but not excessive increase

### Brands / Lines to Explore
| Brand | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Kohler Underscore** | Top candidate — very clean apron, minimal detailing, available in 60"x36", typically $1,200–2,200 |
| **MTI Baths** | US-made, soaking-focused, clean contemporary lines, sold through dealers |
| **Americh** | Similar tier to MTI, worth a look if there's a local dealer |

- Avoid big-box house brands (American Standard, etc.) at this budget — shallower soaking depth, less refined finish
- Material: high-quality acrylic is fine at this price point; cast iron is expensive and rarely available in 36" alcove format
