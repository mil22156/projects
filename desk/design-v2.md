# Floating Corner Desk — Design v2

Supersedes design-notes.md (rosewood panel concept). Same footprint, new structure.
Drafted 2026-06-10.

## Footprint (unchanged)
- L-shaped, NE corner. North run 72" × 30", east run 54" × 30"
- 45° clipped corner spanning full 30" depth (Option C geometry: two slabs, no separate corner piece)
- Fully floating — no legs
- Walls: drywall over wood studs, 16" OC
- Finished height: 28.5"–29" (verify: seated elbow height with arms at 90°)

## Design basis (the numbers)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Dead load (1.5" maple, ~5.5 lb/ft²) | ~130 lbs total |
| Design live load | 200 lb point load anywhere on front edge |
| Worst-case moment at wall | ~5,000 in-lb (200 lb × 28" effective lever) |
| Total design load | ~400 lbs |
| System capacity (6 brackets × 200 lb + ledger) | 1,200+ lbs |

Key principle: **stiffness scales with thickness³.** A 1.5" top is 8× stiffer
than 3/4" — this is what kills front-edge bounce, not bracket count.

The corner zone is self-supporting (two perpendicular walls). The critical
points are the two free ends — west end of north run, south end of east run.
Each free end gets a bracket within 8" of the end, no exceptions.

## Top: 1.5" butcher block

Two sourcing routes:

**Route A — custom 30"-wide slabs (cleaner, pricier)**
- 72" × 30" × 1.5" + 54" × 30" × 1.5", maple or white oak, unfinished
- Custom-size suppliers: butcherblockco.com, countrymouldings.com (instant
  quote calculators), hardwood-lumber.com
- Note: 30" is wider than the stock 25" depth most suppliers carry — order
  custom, expect ~$400–550 per slab

**Route B — rip big-box slabs (cheaper, one extra cut)**
- Big-box stores carry 1.5" × 39"-wide × 72"/96" slabs (hevea, acacia, birch)
  at ~$300–450 each
- Rip to 30" with circular saw + track/straightedge; 9" offcut strips become
  matching floating shelves
- Need two slabs: one 72"+, one 60"+

Either way: acclimate slabs in the office 1–2 weeks before cutting.
Finish: Rubio Monocoat or Osmo Polyx, all six faces (seal the underside and
ends too — uneven moisture exchange = cupping).

## Support system

### 1. Continuous ledger (carries dead weight)
- 1×3 hardwood or 2"×2"×3/16" steel angle along both walls under the back edge
- Lag to EVERY stud: 3/8" × 3.5" structural screws (GRK RSS or equiv), one per stud
- Top of ledger at finished height minus top thickness (e.g., 27.25" for a
  28.75" surface with 1.5" top)

### 2. Steel cantilever brackets (fight the moment)
- Type: **surface-mount floating countertop bracket** — flat steel arm
  ~1/2" × 2.5" × 24–26" welded to a vertical backplate. The backplate lags to
  the face of the wall into studs.
- ⚠️ NOT the "inside-wall" type (requires open framing — walls here are finished)
- ⚠️ NOT rod-style concealed shelf brackets (built for 10–12" shelves; they bend
  or wallow out at 30" depth)
- Rating: 200+ lb per bracket at this depth. Sources: ironsupports.com
  (200 lb/bracket, 1/2" steel), The Original Granite Bracket (Amazon),
  Shelfology Drake, A&M Hardware. ~$25–45 each.
- Mounting: two 3/8" structural screws per backplate, into stud, 3" thread
  engagement minimum
- Arms sit in 1/2"-deep × 2.5"-wide dados routed into the slab underside
  (router + straightedge, or careful multiple passes with circular saw + chisel)
- Fasten slab to arms from below with pan-head screws through the arm slots
  (slotted holes across the grain = wood movement allowance)

### 3. Bracket layout (finalize after stud mapping)

| Location | Count | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| North run free end (west) | 1 | within 8" of end — closest stud |
| North run field | 2 | roughly every other stud (~32" OC) |
| East run free end (south) | 1 | within 8" of end — closest stud |
| East run field | 1–2 | every other stud |
| Corner zone (within ~30" of corner) | 0 | ledgers on both walls carry it |

Total: 5–6 brackets.

## Corner join
- Two slabs, each with a 45° cut forming the clipped corner (Option C)
- Join underneath with 2× countertop draw-bolts (aka miter bolts/dogbones) +
  3 dominos or biscuits for alignment
- Router template or careful jigsaw + flush-trim for the draw-bolt recesses
- Assemble in place on the ledgers, draw tight, then screw down to bracket arms
- Hairline 45° seam at the corner; from the front the north run reads as one piece

## Build sequence
1. Map studs on both walls (stud finder + small nail test holes where ledger
   will hide them). Mark centers on painter's tape. Update this doc with positions.
2. Finalize bracket positions against real stud locations.
3. Order/buy slabs; acclimate 1–2 weeks.
4. Cut slabs to length, cut the two 45° corner cuts.
5. Route bracket dados + draw-bolt recesses on undersides. Dry-fit the joint
   face down on the floor.
6. Finish all faces (2 coats, light sand between).
7. Mount ledgers level (laser or long level — the two walls must agree).
8. Mount brackets, shimming backplates plumb if drywall is wavy.
9. Set slabs, draw the corner bolt-tight, screw to bracket arms from below.
10. Sit on the front edge. It shouldn't care.

## Rejected approaches (and why)
- Rod-style concealed brackets — shelf hardware, fails at 30" leverage
- Any 3/4" top — density ≠ stiffness; bouncy at this depth regardless of species
- Full-miter corner (old Option A) — unforgiving cut, fragile glue line, no benefit
- Torsion box — structurally excellent but heavy on shop labor for the same result
- Inside-wall brackets — would require opening up finished drywall
