# Desk Design Notes

## Specs
- Location: NE corner of office (15.5' N/S x 17' E/W)
- North run: ~6' (72") from NE corner going west
- East run: ~4.5' (54") from NE corner going south
- Depth: 30" all around
- Corner: diagonal cut (45°)
- Style: floating/wall-mounted
- Material: 3/4" Bolivian rosewood pre-glued panels (8'x4' sheets)

---

## Corner Design Options

### Option A: Mitered join (cleanest look)
- North slab: full 72" long, east end cut at 45°
- East slab: full 54" long, north end cut at 45°
- The two angled ends meet at the corner — no separate corner piece
- Seam runs diagonally from the walls to ~42" out from the corner
- Pros: two pieces total, strong miter seam, grain flows continuously
- Cons: requires accurate 45° cuts across a 30" wide slab

### Option B: Separate corner triangle
- Corner piece: right triangle, 30" legs, hypotenuse ~42.4"
- North slab: 42" x 30" (starts 30" west of east wall)
- East slab: 24" x 30" (starts 30" south of north wall)
- Three pieces sit side by side on ledger boards
- Pros: simpler cuts, easy to install independently
- Cons: two visible seam lines, shorter runs feel less substantial

### Option C: Full north slab + separate east slab (most common)
- North slab: full 72" x 30", with SW corner of NE end cut at 45°
- East slab: 54" x 30", with NW corner cut at 45° to butt cleanly against north slab
- Corner is formed by the two diagonal cuts meeting
- Pros: north slab looks like one unbroken piece from the front, good for monitor/main work area
- Cons: slightly more complex fitting at the join

**Recommendation: Option A or C**

---

## Panel Cutting Layout

### Panel 1 (8'x4' = 96"x48")
- Rip to 30" wide along full 96" length
- Cut north slab from this: 72" long (leaves 24" offcut — useful for ledger backer or shelf)
- Offcut: 24" x 30"

### Panel 2 (8'x4' = 96"x48")
- Rip to 30" wide
- Cut east slab: 54" long (leaves 42" offcut)
- Offcut: 42" x 30" — enough to cut a corner triangle if going Option B

**Total: 2 panels** (with useful offcuts)

## Floating Support Design

### Ledger board method
- 2x4 or 2x6 ledger boards screwed into wall studs along north and east walls
- Ledger sits at desk height minus 3/4" (to account for slab thickness)
- Standard desk height: 29"–30" finished surface
- Ledgers must hit studs (typically 16" OC) — use stud finder before planning screw layout

### Front support
Bolivian rosewood is dense (~55 lbs/ft³). A 30"x72" slab at 3/4" = ~47 lbs. The front edge will cantilever off the ledger — need support:
- **Option 1**: Hidden steel angle brackets (L-brackets) at front corners, painted to match wall
- **Option 2**: Single hairpin leg at the outer front corner of each run (partially defeats floating look but very strong)
- **Option 3**: Steel rod corbels welded to a wall plate — industrial/modern look
- **Option 4**: Full floating with heavy-duty concealed shelf brackets rated 100+ lbs each, spaced ~24"

Recommended: concealed heavy-duty shelf brackets + ledger along walls. With 30" depth and dense wood, use brackets rated for at least 150 lbs each, spaced no more than 24" apart.

---

## Notes on Bolivian Rosewood
- Very dense, hard wood — difficult to work with hand tools, wears blades fast
- Pre-glued panels: check grain direction and glue line quality before buying
- Sands and finishes beautifully — oil finish (Rubio Monocoat or Osmo) brings out color well
- Will need pre-drilling for all fasteners to avoid splitting
- Heavy: factor this into wall mounting — make sure to hit studs, not just drywall
